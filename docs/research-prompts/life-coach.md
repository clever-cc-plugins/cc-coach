# Research Prompt: life-coach

**For:** `cc-coach:life-coach`
**Current knowledge base:** `plugins/cc-coach/skills/_shared/life-coaching-framework.md` —
Wheel of Life, GROW, GTD-derived follow-through techniques, A Perfect Mess's
"optimal disorder" counterbalance, the F.A.C.E. resilience framework, and a
perfectionism domain from _The Perfectionist's Guide to Losing Control_. All
practitioner-book sourced; no peer-reviewed backing doc yet, and this is the
skill with the most direct proximity to mental-health-adjacent territory.
**Why research helps:** validate the behavior-change techniques against actual
psychology research, and get clearer, better-sourced guidance on where
coaching should stop and professional referral should start.

Paste the prompt below into a deep-research AI tool (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini,
Perplexity, or similar). Save the response as Markdown, e.g. to
`docs/research/life-coach.md`, then use it to refine
`life-coaching-framework.md`.

```
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Research prompt: life coaching and behavior change — evidence base and scope
─────────────────────────────────────────────
You are a research analyst investigating the evidence base for life coaching
techniques and the appropriate professional boundary between life coaching
and mental-health treatment, for use in an AI coaching tool aimed at
non-clinical personal goals (habits, work-life balance, general life
direction).

Cover, with headers separating each area:

1. The Wheel of Life tool — origin, evidence for its usefulness as a
   diagnostic/prioritization tool versus its status as a popular but
   informal coaching exercise, and any research-backed alternatives for
   helping someone identify where to focus.
2. Habit-formation and behavior-change research (e.g. work building on the
   habit-loop / implementation-intentions literature, self-determination
   theory) as it applies to a life-coaching Will-stage commitment — what
   actually predicts whether a person follows through on a personally
   generated goal.
3. Research on perfectionism as a coaching-relevant (not clinical) construct
   — the adaptive/maladaptive perfectionism distinction specifically:
   current research consensus, and how confidently a non-clinical coaching
   tool can rely on that distinction.
4. **Explicit scope research**: published guidance (from coaching
   professional bodies — e.g. ICF — and from psychology/psychiatry sources)
   on where life coaching's appropriate scope ends and a referral to mental
   health treatment should begin. What are the recognized warning signs a
   coaching conversation should not continue past, and what is the
   recommended way to hand off?
5. Research on the "cost of organizing"/optimal-disorder idea from
   Abrahamson & Freedman's _A Perfect Mess_ — is there supporting research
   in organizational or personal-productivity literature for the idea that
   moderate disorder can outperform maximal organization, or is this mostly
   an unverified popular-science claim?

Require credible, verifiable sources throughout, with in-text citations —
prioritize peer-reviewed psychology research and recognized coaching-body
guidance (e.g. International Coaching Federation) over general
self-help/coaching-industry content. Be explicit about item 4 in particular —
this is the most safety-relevant part of the research. Provide a formatted
source list in APA style at the end. Respond in Markdown.
─────────────────────────────────────────────
```
