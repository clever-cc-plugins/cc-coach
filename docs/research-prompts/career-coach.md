# Research Prompt: career-coach

**For:** `cc-coach:career-coach`
**Current knowledge base:** `plugins/cc-coach/skills/_shared/career-coaching-framework.md` —
the GROW model, plus a networking domain distilled from Ferrazzi's _Never Eat
Alone_, Wolf's _Networking – Dictyonomie_, Petrilli's _Introvert's Guide_, and
Carnegie's _How to Win Friends and Influence People_, and a cross-reference to
the F.A.C.E. resilience framework. All practitioner-book sourced; no
peer-reviewed backing doc yet.
**Why research helps:** GROW is widely used but its formal evidence base is
thinner than its popularity suggests — worth knowing precisely what's
supported and what to caveat.

Paste the prompt below into a deep-research AI tool (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini,
Perplexity, or similar). Save the response as Markdown, e.g. to
`docs/research/career-coach.md`, then use it to refine
`career-coaching-framework.md`.

```
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Research prompt: career coaching — evidence base and best practices
─────────────────────────────────────────────
You are a research analyst investigating the evidence base and best practices
for one-on-one career coaching conversations, for use in an AI coaching tool.

Cover, with headers separating each area:

1. The GROW model (Goal, Reality, Options, Will) — origin (Graham Alexander,
   Alan Fine, Sir John Whitmore), and what coaching-psychology research says
   about its actual effectiveness versus its popularity. Are there
   controlled studies, and what do they show?
2. Broader coaching-psychology research on what makes a coaching
   conversation effective — e.g. the role of the coaching relationship
   itself (working alliance), powerful questioning technique, and
   goal-setting theory (Locke & Latham) as it applies inside a live
   conversation rather than a written plan.
3. Evidence-based practice for the "Options" stage specifically — research
   on solution-generation techniques that avoid the coach's bias toward
   their own suggestions (e.g. structured brainstorming, avoiding premature
   convergence).
4. Evidence-based practice for the "Will" stage / commitment devices —
   research on what makes a person actually follow through on a
   self-generated commitment (implementation intentions research — Gollwitzer
   — is directly relevant here).
5. Career-coaching-specific research (as distinct from general life/executive
   coaching) — what's known about coaching people through career transitions,
   job searches, or promotion pushes specifically.
6. Networking research — what does organizational-behavior/social-capital
   research (e.g. work building on Granovetter's "strength of weak ties," or
   more recent network-building research) add beyond the practitioner-book
   advice already in this tool (give-first reciprocity, weak-tie cultivation,
   the value of "anchor" connectors)?

Require credible, verifiable sources throughout, with in-text citations —
prioritize peer-reviewed research over coaching-industry marketing content,
and be explicit where a popular technique (like GROW) has thinner formal
evidence than its popularity suggests. Provide a formatted source list in APA
style at the end. Respond in Markdown.
─────────────────────────────────────────────
```
