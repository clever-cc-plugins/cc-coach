# Research Prompt: presentation-coach

**For:** `cc-coach:presentation-coach`
**Current knowledge base:** `plugins/cc-coach/skills/_shared/presentation-coaching-framework.md` —
a five-point presentation quality rubric, the SBI feedback model, and
Cialdini's persuasion/pre-suasion principles adapted for live talks. The SBI
model and rubric are applied from general knowledge; Cialdini's portion
already has a peer-reviewed research base (see `cc-content`'s
`persuasion-principles.md`) but hasn't been re-verified for the live-talk
adaptation specifically.
**Why research helps:** validate the rubric and SBI model against
communication-science and public-speaking research, and check the live-talk
adaptation of persuasion principles against research on spoken (not written)
persuasion specifically.

Paste the prompt below into a deep-research AI tool (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini,
Perplexity, or similar). Save the response as Markdown, e.g. to
`docs/research/presentation-coach.md`, then use it to refine
`presentation-coaching-framework.md`.

---

```
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Research prompt: public speaking and presentation coaching — evidence base
─────────────────────────────────────────────
You are a research analyst investigating the evidence base and best practices
for coaching individuals on talks, presentations, and public speaking, for
use in an AI coaching tool.

Cover, with headers separating each area:

1. Research on what structurally distinguishes a well-received talk from a
   poorly received one — audience-comprehension and audience-engagement
   research (e.g. work on narrative structure in communication, cognitive
   load in live audiences) that would validate or extend a rubric currently
   covering: message clarity, structure/opening, evidence, delivery, and
   audience fit.
2. The SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact) feedback model — origin (Center for
   Creative Leadership), evidence for its effectiveness versus other
   feedback models (e.g. SBI-I with Intent, radical candor, feedforward),
   and whether it's specifically validated for presentation/speaking
   feedback or more general workplace feedback.
3. Public-speaking-specific anxiety and delivery research — what's actually
   known about reducing filler words, improving pacing, and building
   presence, distinct from general confidence advice.
4. Research on persuasion specifically in spoken/live delivery as opposed to
   written content — does audience visibility of the speaker change which
   persuasion principles work or backfire (e.g. research on nonverbal
   congruence, vocal confidence cues, live audience skepticism-detection)?
5. Evidence on rehearsal methods — what does research say actually improves
   a talk between drafting and delivery (e.g. deliberate practice research
   applied to public speaking, video self-review effectiveness)?

Require credible, verifiable sources throughout, with in-text citations —
prioritize peer-reviewed communication-science research and well-established
sources (e.g. Toastmasters research partnerships, TED's own published
findings) over generic public-speaking-blog content. Provide a formatted
source list in APA style at the end. Respond in Markdown.
─────────────────────────────────────────────
```
